BRING YOUR OWN SIGNAL

Connect a data source.

Monitor your laptop’s real Wi-Fi signal through the local reader. Spatial sensing requires additional hardware.

Run the local reader

Download the project from GitHub. In its folder, start these in separate terminals:

python3 tools/bridge.pypython3 tools/wifi_source.py

Open http://127.0.0.1:8765 ↗, then choose Connect sensor → Local bridge. macOS needs Apple’s Swift compiler; Linux needs a Wi-Fi driver reporting dBm. Phone browsers and Windows are unsupported.

This measures your connected Wi-Fi signal. It does not detect people, estimate distance, or create 3D positions.

A website cannot read your laptop’s Wi-Fi radio directly. The local reader measures signal strength; raw CSI needs compatible sensors. Real 3D targets require externally estimated coordinates.

THE SIGNAL, EXPLAINED

A new view of Wi-Fi.

Inspired by RuView, NutRadar is an open-source 3D interface for exploring Wi-Fi sensing data.

01
Explore immediately

The demo generates synthetic tracks, CSI-like waves, and virtual nodes. It does not scan your surroundings.

02
Connect your hardware

Compatible firmware can stream raw CSI over USB or through the free local bridge. Hardware is purchased separately.

03
Bring estimated positions

Visualize 3D coordinates from your own calibrated localization pipeline. NutRadar itself does not reconstruct people from raw CSI.
